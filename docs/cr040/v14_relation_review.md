## Cross-cutting points

- **Relevance to the textbook domain.** None of the six relations exists in the production registry v1.3, and the four evaluation relations come from scientific-paper benchmarks (SciNLP). A networking textbook names metrics and testbeds rarely. If registry v1.4 is adopted for the textbook, the `evaluation` family adds three choices to the family step on every pair; that has a cost in precision which the CR-022 Part B transfer run would measure. Whether the evaluation family belongs in the textbook registry at all is an owner decision.
- **Prompts.** The relation prompts (relation_family v3 to v5, relation_choice v4 to v7, relation_qualifiers v4 to v6) were written for v1.3 families. They list families from the registry, but the rules text does not explain the evaluation family or similar_to. Adopting v1.4 needs new prompt versions (rule 8).
- **The comparison family.** With similar_to the comparison family offers contrasts_with, similar_to, same_concept, no_relation and other. similar_to and same_concept are easy to confuse (alike vs the same thing); the near-misses above are the only guard until the prompt says more.

### similar_to

Family `comparison`, layer `semantic`, directional False, symmetric True, transitive False, inverse `None`, SciNLP label(s) `similarWith` (unchanged by this proposal).

| field | current (v1.4) | proposed | reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| definition | Source and target are alike in a stated respect but are DIFFERENT concepts (never a merge or an alias; same-concept equivalence stays a node property, CR-008). | Source and target are alike in a stated or clearly implied respect (a shared property, role or mechanism) but are DIFFERENT concepts. | template | {X} is similar to {Y} | (unchanged) | Unchanged. |
| examples | - Adam similar_to RMSprop<br>- UDP similar_to raw IP datagrams | - Adam similar_to RMSprop<br>- bit stuffing similar_to byte stuffing | Replaces "UDP similar_to raw IP datagrams" (UDP is a protocol and a raw datagram is a data unit, so the likeness is loose) with a networking pair that is alike as framing techniques. |
