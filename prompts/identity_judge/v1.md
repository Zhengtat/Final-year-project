You decide how one TERM from a technical text relates to one existing CONCEPT in a knowledge graph. A program has already checked the names, the
lexicon, abbreviations and textbook alias statements and could not decide, so you decide from the sentence and the concept's description only.

Answer with exactly one of these five words in the field `decision`:

- same: the TERM and the CONCEPT are one and the same concept: a synonym, an abbreviation, a spelling or word-order variant, a plural, or a form
  that adds only a word that does not change the meaning here. Use this test: the CONCEPT's name could replace the TERM in this sentence, and the
  TERM could replace the CONCEPT everywhere else, without changing what is meant. Only then answer same.
- narrower: the TERM is a narrower concept than the CONCEPT: a kind, an instance, a part, a variant or a specialisation of it. It adds a word that
  makes it a narrower kind, a measure, a part, a role or a property of the CONCEPT.
- broader: the TERM is a broader concept than the CONCEPT: the CONCEPT is a kind, an instance, a part or a specialisation of the TERM.
- new: the TERM is a real concept of its own that is neither the same as the CONCEPT nor narrower or broader than it (a different idea, even if
  it is related or mentioned together with the CONCEPT).
- not_a_concept: the TERM is not a technical concept in this sentence (a person, a generic everyday word used loosely, a fragment).

Rules:
- A shorter or longer name alone does not decide the answer. Never answer same only because the words overlap or because the two are often
  mentioned together.
- When you are unsure between same and narrower or broader, answer narrower or broader (or new). A wrong same merges two concepts for good.
- Judge the TERM as it is used in the sentence. Do not use outside knowledge to merge two things the sentence treats as different.
- `evidence_quote` must be an EXACT substring of the sentence (copy it character for character) that shows what the TERM refers to.
- `reason`: one sentence. `confidence`: a number from 0 to 1.

Examples (a different domain, only to show the five answers):
- TERM "automobile", CONCEPT "car", sentence "An automobile has four wheels." -> same
- TERM "repair of engines", CONCEPT "engine repair" -> same
- TERM "sports car", CONCEPT "car", sentence "A sports car is built for speed." -> narrower
- TERM "engine block", CONCEPT "engine" -> narrower
- TERM "vehicle", CONCEPT "car", sentence "Every vehicle needs fuel or a battery." -> broader
- TERM "driver", CONCEPT "vehicle", sentence "The driver steers the vehicle." -> new
- TERM "thing", CONCEPT "car", sentence "The thing was parked outside." -> not_a_concept
