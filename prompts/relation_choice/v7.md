You are picking the exact relation a sentence states between two concepts, within the family already identified for this pair.

Rules for every family except misconception:
- Pick the reading the sentence actually states. If the sentence states OR DENIES a relation, choose that relation: a denial is recorded by the
  polarity qualifier in a later step, so it is not a reason to choose no_relation or other.
- If X and Y are only listed together or co-mentioned, choose no_relation.
- If the sentence says X and Y are two names for the very same thing (a synonym, an alias, "also called"), and the options include same_concept,
  choose same_concept. Do not choose it when the sentence says they differ, or when they are only similar or related.
- Each option shows its reading filled in with the two concepts. direction is "forward" for a reading that names X first, "reversed" for a reading
  that names Y first; send "forward" for symmetric relations, no_relation and other.
- Choose other only if the sentence relates X and Y but no option fits. Then other_description and other_suggested_label are REQUIRED. For every
  other answer both are null.
- For contrasts_with, dimension_quote is REQUIRED: one contiguous piece of the sentence, copied character for character, that names what X and Y
  differ in (for example "faster"). If the sentence names no such words, choose no_relation instead. For every other relation it is null.
- evidence_quote: an EXACT substring of the sentence that states the relation and contains both X and Y (or their names).
- statement: a one-sentence paraphrase of the relation in your own words, naming both concepts.

Rules for the misconception family only (the family is misconception when the sentence presents a belief as wrong):
- is_misconception: true only if the sentence warns against a wrong belief about X and Y. If it merely denies a relation or contrasts the two
  plainly, answer false (the other fields are then empty: wrong_relation is "none").
- wrong_relation, wrong_direction, wrong_polarity: the WRONG statement the learner might believe, as a registry relation between X and Y (or
  conflated_with when the learner mixes the two concepts up), in the reading direction of the options.
- perturbation_type: how the wrong statement differs from the correct one: polarity_flip, reversed, substituted_concept, conflation,
  wrong_category, wrong_relation, condition_error or modality_error.
- correct_relation, correct_direction, correct_polarity: the CORRECT statement if the sentence gives it, otherwise correct_relation is "none".
- misconception_quote: an EXACT substring of the sentence that shows the wrong belief. correction_quote: an EXACT substring of the sentence that
  gives the correction, or null when the sentence gives none.
- prevalence_cue: stated_common (the text says many people believe it), stated_possible (the text says one might) or none.
- reason: one sentence.
