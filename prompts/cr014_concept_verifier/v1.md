You are verifying candidate terms from one section of a {domain} textbook. A program listed many candidate spans: most are
NOT concepts. Judge every candidate on its own, from the sentence(s) shown, and give exactly one verdict.

A concept is a single word or short phrase that names an essential knowledge element of the field and has a specific meaning
in it. A word that is also an everyday word IS a concept when the field gives it a specific technical meaning. The whole
term as the text uses it must be the candidate.

Verdicts:
- ACCEPT_NEW: the candidate, exactly as written, is a standalone concept the text names or uses.
- LINK_EXISTING: it is the same concept as one of the EXISTING MATCHES listed on its line (interchangeable in any sentence of
  the book). Give that node_id. A kind, part, instance or version is NOT the same.
- REJECT_NOT_CONCEPT: a clause, a list, a value (number, size, date), a descriptive or evaluative phrase, a sentence fragment.
- REJECT_GENERIC: a generic discourse or everyday word with no specific technical meaning here ("approach", "case", "way", "example").
- REJECT_PARTIAL_OR_WRONG_BOUNDARY: only part of a longer term the text uses, or longer than the term (it includes extra
  words), so the span as written is not the concept. (If the same words also stand alone as a concept elsewhere in the
  sentence, the verdict is ACCEPT_NEW.)
- INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE: the sentence(s) shown do not let you decide.

Rules:
- Return exactly one verdict per candidate_id, in the same order.
- node_id is the id from the candidate's EXISTING MATCHES for LINK_EXISTING and null for every other verdict.
- reason_code is one of: valid_term, existing_node, generic_word, descriptive_phrase, value_or_quantity, fragment_or_clause,
  part_of_longer_term, overlong, unclear_context.
- Do not reward a candidate for being frequent or for being found by several proposers; judge the term itself.
- When unsure between a concept and a non-concept, choose INSUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE rather than guessing ACCEPT_NEW.
