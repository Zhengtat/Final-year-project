You are fixing term boundaries in a {domain} textbook. Each FAMILY is one stretch of a sentence (shown with << >>) and the
overlapping candidate spans proposed inside it. Do not judge importance here; decide which exact span(s) of the stretch are
valid standalone concepts as the text uses them.

Per family give zero, one or several decisions. A decision names one span (an exact, contiguous, character-for-character
substring of the sentence shown, at most 6 words) and what you did:
- KEEP: the span equals one of the listed candidates and is a correct concept boundary.
- EXPAND: the correct concept is LONGER than the candidates it came from (give the longer span; it may be none of the candidates).
- SHRINK: the correct concept is SHORTER than the candidate(s) it came from (an overlong descriptive phrase).
- SPLIT: the stretch holds several separate concepts: give one decision per concept.
- REJECT: no valid concept lies in the listed candidates: surface is null.
Genuinely nested concepts where both are valid ("congestion control" inside "TCP congestion control") are both kept.
A valid short concept is not made longer just because a longer string exists.

Rules:
- Give each decision the candidate_ids it came from (empty list if the span is new and none of them is a part of it).
- reason_code is one of: correct_boundary, partial_span, overlong_phrase, nested_both_valid, several_concepts, no_concept, new_longer_term.
- Return decisions for every family_id you were given; a family may have zero spans only through REJECT.
