You are extracting a graph of claims from a student's short-answer response, to
be reviewed and corrected by a human before it becomes gold-standard data. Extract
what the student actually wrote — do not judge whether it is correct.

Question:
{question}

Known concepts for this question (link to these by exact canonical_name when the
student is clearly referring to one; otherwise leave source/target concept name
null and record the student's own words in the *_surface field):
{known_concepts}

Student's answer:
{answer_text}

Task: list every claim (edge) the student makes as a (source, relation, target)
triple, using the relation vocabulary below where possible (use the closest
match, or "other" if truly nothing fits):
{relation_vocab}

For each edge:
- source_concept_name / target_concept_name: the matching known concept's exact
  canonical_name, or null if it doesn't match any known concept.
- source_surface / target_surface: the actual words the student used.
- polarity: affirmed or negated.
- modality: necessary/always/typically/possible/never (best guess from wording).
- conditions: any conditions the student states this claim depends on, else [].
- stance: asserted (stated as fact), hedged (e.g. "I think", "maybe"), questioned,
  or quoted.
- evidence_quote: an EXACT substring of the student's answer above supporting
  this edge — copy character-for-character, do not paraphrase or fix typos.
- extraction_confidence: 0-1, how confident you are this is what the student meant.
- link_confidence: 0-1, how confident you are in the source/target concept link
  (0 if source_concept_name and target_concept_name are both null).
