You are annotating the discourse structure of a passage from a computer-networking textbook, in the style of eRST
(enhanced Rhetorical Structure Theory). The passage is split into numbered units; each unit is one sentence.

Task: list the discourse relations that hold between units of this passage. Each relation links nucleus unit(s) and, for
most relations, one satellite unit. Only report a relation when the text itself signals it (a connective such as "because"
or "however", a lexical cue, a reference, a syntactic construction, a list or colon, repetition, ...). If two units are
merely next to each other, report nothing for them. Reporting fewer, well-supported relations is better than reporting many.

Relation inventory (choose the label EXACTLY; nothing else is allowed):
{relation_options}

Nuclearity key: "satellite after nucleus" = the satellite unit comes later in the text than its nucleus; "satellite before
nucleus" = earlier; "either order" = the satellite may come before or after; "multinuclear" = two or more equally
prominent nuclei and NO satellite.

Signal types you may attach to a relation (a signal is the text evidence for the relation):
{signal_options}

Rules:
1. Use only unit ids shown below. A relation connects units of this passage; a unit can take part in several relations.
2. For a satellite relation give exactly one nucleus id in `nucleus_units`, the satellite id in `satellite_unit`, and say
   whether the satellite is `before` or `after` the nucleus in the text. For a multinuclear relation give two or more
   nucleus ids, `satellite_unit` = null and `satellite_position` = null.
3. `evidence` is a quote copied EXACTLY (same words and punctuation) from the text of the units it connects. It must be a
   contiguous piece of one unit or of two joined units. Do not paraphrase.
4. Each signal needs an `anchor_text` copied exactly from the text (the words that signal the relation), except a layout
   signal, which has none. `subtype` must be one of the listed subtypes of its kind, and null for a discourse marker.
5. Do not describe relations between concepts of the domain (is-a, part-of, uses, ...). Describe how the TEXT is organised:
   one sentence explains, elaborates, contrasts with, gives a reason for, or gives an example of another.
6. `confidence` is your own estimate from 0 to 1.
7. If no relation is signalled in this passage, return an empty list.

Passage (section {section_id}: {heading}); units {first_unit} to {last_unit}:
{units}
